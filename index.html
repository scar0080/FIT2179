Climate Change and Natural Hazards in Australia

Australia's climate has changed significantly over the past century. Rising temperatures, warming oceans, changing rainfall patterns and increasing fire weather conditions affect ecosystems, communities, infrastructure and disaster risk.

Introduction

Climate change influences multiple components of Australia's environment. This report presents long-term trends in air temperature, sea surface temperature, rainfall and fire weather conditions before exploring the distribution of natural disaster events across the country.

Air Temperature

Australia's average temperature has increased substantially since national records began.

Temperature warming stripes showing the transition from cooler to warmer years.

Why this matters

  • Heatwaves are becoming more frequent and intense.
  • Higher temperatures increase heat-related illness.
  • Agriculture experiences greater heat and water stress.
  • Energy demand increases during extreme heat events.

Source: Bureau of Meteorology, CSIRO State of the Climate (2024).

Sea Temperature

Sea surface temperature warming stripes.

Why this matters

  • Marine heatwaves are becoming more common.
  • Coral bleaching events increase under prolonged heat stress.
  • Fish distributions may shift as ocean temperatures change.
  • Warmer oceans provide additional atmospheric moisture.

Source: Bureau of Meteorology, CSIRO State of the Climate (2024), Australian Institute of Marine Science.

Fire Weather Conditions

Fire weather combines temperature, humidity, wind and drought conditions. Dangerous fire weather days have increased across many regions of Australia over recent decades.

Why this matters

  • Hot and dry conditions increase fuel flammability.
  • Severe bushfires can spread more rapidly.
  • Communities face greater risks from extreme fire events.
  • Environmental recovery may take decades.

Source: Bureau of Meteorology, CSIRO.

Rainfall

Climate change affects both average rainfall and the intensity of heavy rainfall events. Warmer air can hold more moisture, increasing the potential for intense rainfall.

Why this matters

  • Drought risk increases in some regions.
  • Extreme rainfall contributes to flood impacts.
  • Water resource planning becomes more challenging.
  • Ecosystems respond to changes in rainfall availability.

Source: Bureau of Meteorology, CSIRO Climate Change Q&A.
